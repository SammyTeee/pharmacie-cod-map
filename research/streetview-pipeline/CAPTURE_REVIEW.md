# First route: observations from reviewed screenshots

These are visual observations from the local originals/contact sheets, not
measurements or a complete building inventory. Open the local gallery described
in FIREFOX_CAPTURE.md and search the image IDs. Review remaining views before
modelling. Passing vehicles, people, reflections and Google overlays are not
architecture. April2016, April2019, April2023, August2024 and April2026 imagery occurs in this set;
changes in shop occupancy must be compared rather than silently combined.

## Brook and bridge — 00073–00092

Metal vertical-bar railings line the pavement above a narrow watercourse. The
brook has vegetated banks and trees, with low brick structures and walls nearby.
Several views show the bridge edge, pavement width, roadway and junction together.
Corner brick retail buildings have lower shop glazing/signs beneath upper windows.
The Golden Barber corner/side street is visible around00089. Cars and pedestrians
obscure parts of the lower frontage; use adjacent cameras. Railings/watercourse
should be modelled independently, not baked into a shopfront photo.

## Older shop row — 00126

The reviewed native view explicitly says April2016. Today's Catch has a blue sign
with large white wording and a smaller menu line; glazed frontage/transom below,
an inset glazed entry and pale low panels. Age UK has a white fascia with large
blue lettering/coloured emblem, turquoise lower frames and pale plinth. Mind has
a green sign, green lower framing and display glazing. Continuous red-brick upper
storey has rectangular windows, differing blinds/frames, dark gutter and pitched
roof with an aerial. Treat shop names as historical. This particular intended
left-detail view correctly shows the west shop row. Images00126/00128 retain a
stale URL heading from the earlier camera state; visual review overrides that
reported heading. Their original metadata is preserved and annotated separately.

## Main retail stretch — 00145–00168

Food Warehouse/cardfactory/Savers side: broad red-brick mass, large signs above
lower glazing, repeated small upper openings/sign panels, low barriers and level
changes at the footway. Opposite row includes Subway, William Hill and adjoining
units, with repeated rectangular upper windows and individually framed shop bays.
Next camera shows Greggs, Byrite electricals and Smith Hamylton wording on the
opposite side, plus the DIY frontage and its exposed brick gable. Crossing signal
posts, kerb edges, white road markings and lamp columns supply roadside context.
Sign transcriptions should be checked against native originals before typesetting.

## B&M / shopping centre / Costa stretch — 00222–00225

Reviewed detail00222 shows a substantial red-brick facade with projecting piers,
gables, dentil-like brickwork, dark vertical rainwater pipes and an upper bay with
a Y-shaped frame. A pair of smaller upper windows is recessed below brick arches.
Blue/orange B&M signage contrasts with the brick. Market canopies, parked cars and
a van obscure the lower frontage; they do not establish the wall/door shape behind.
Forward00225 shows Costa opposite the shopping-centre row, continuous pavement,
lamp column, street parking and the roundabout approach. Its panel says April2026.

## Reconstruction use

Use the wide/oblique views for building order, massing and relative depths; tighter
views for frames, signs, sills, cornices and plinths. Street-normal shots are useful
candidates, not guaranteed orthographic facades. Fit signs/windows as separate
geometry/materials where practical, following the successful v19–v24 approach.
Camera GeoJSON provides geographic reference markers only. Calibrate the existing
gameplay-enlarged Blender map separately before moving buildings to match it.
